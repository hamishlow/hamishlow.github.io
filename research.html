Research

My work examines individual risk and insurance over the life cycle, using structural life-cycle models with uncertainty and structural estimation. Full publication list available in the CV.

Fertility & Family

  • Fertility and Family Labor Supply
    Jakobsen, T. N., Jørgensen, T. H., & Low, H.
    Forthcoming, American Economic Review
    NBER PDF
    We examine how fertility decisions and family labor supply interact in response to economic incentives. Using a structural life-cycle model, we identify the dynamic complementarities between childbearing timing, maternal labor force participation, and household income over the life cycle.
  • Marriage, Labor Supply and the Dynamics of the Social Safety Net
    Low, H., Meghir, C., Pistaferri, L., & Voena, A.
    2023  ·  NBER WP 24356 R&R (2nd round) — Journal of Political Economy
    NBER
    We build a structural model of marriage, divorce, and labor supply to study how the design of the social safety net shapes marriage markets and female labor supply over the life cycle. We show that the interaction between welfare programs and family formation creates important dynamic incentives that are missed by static analyses.
  • Joint Child Custody and Interstate Migration
    Adams, G., Bayraktar, S., Jørgensen, T. H., Low, H., & Voena, A.
    2025
    We study how joint custody arrangements after divorce affect the geographic mobility of divorced parents. Using U.S. data, we show that joint custody significantly reduces interstate migration among divorced parents with children, with important implications for labor market outcomes and child welfare.

Housing, Durables & Wealth

  • Durables and Lemons: Private Information and the Market for Cars
    Blundell, R., Gu, J., Leth-Petersen, S., Low, H., & Meghir, C.
    2023  ·  NBER WP 26281 R&R (2nd round) — Quantitative Economics
    NBER
    We study the used car market, where private information about vehicle quality creates adverse selection (the "lemons" problem). Using a structural model combined with rich Danish administrative data on households and vehicle transactions, we estimate the extent of private information and its welfare consequences.
  • House Price Rises and Borrowing to Invest
    Crossley, T., Levell, P., & Low, H.
    Forthcoming, Journal of Economic Behavior & Organisation, 2024
    We study how rising house prices affect household borrowing behavior. Using UK data, we show that households use housing equity gains as collateral to borrow and invest in financial assets and home improvements, amplifying the wealth effects of house price appreciation.
  • Labour Supply and the Cost of House Price Booms and Busts
    Low, H. & Sanchez-Marcos, V.
    Forthcoming, Labour Economics, 2024
    We estimate the impact of house price fluctuations on household labor supply. Using a structural life-cycle model, we show how boom-bust cycles in housing markets generate heterogeneous labor supply responses across the wealth distribution.
  • Why Do Home Owners Work Longer Hours?
    Bottazzi, R., Low, H., & Wakefield, M.
    IFS Working Paper 07-10, 2009
    We document that home owners work significantly more hours than renters, and investigate the mechanisms behind this relationship. We show this is not explained by selection; instead, housing tenure creates incentives to accumulate more income through greater labor supply in order to service mortgage commitments.

Disability & Insurance

  • Disability Insurance: Error Rates and Gender Differences
    Low, H. & Pistaferri, L.
    Conditional accept, Journal of Political Economy, 2024 Conditional Accept
    We quantify the rates at which disability insurance applications are wrongly rejected (false negatives) and wrongly approved (false positives), and examine whether these error rates differ by gender. We find significant gender differences in screening outcomes that are not fully explained by differences in underlying health conditions.
  • Outcome Tests and Screening Errors
    Low, H. & Pistaferri, L.
    2025
    We develop outcome-based tests to detect and quantify screening errors in administrative benefit programs. We apply our framework to disability insurance to identify the extent to which screening is miscalibrated, and to assess the welfare costs of these errors for applicants.
  • The Idiosyncratic Impact of an Aggregate Shock: The Distributional Consequences of COVID-19
    Benzeval, M., Burton, J., Crossley, T. F., Fisher, P., Jäckle, A., Low, H., & Read, B.
    Oxford Discussion Paper 911, 2020
    We document the heterogeneous distributional consequences of the COVID-19 shock across the income distribution. Using high-frequency survey data, we show that the aggregate shock had very different impacts on households depending on their prior financial situation, employment type, and family composition.

Consumption, Savings & Inequality

  • Consumption and Income Inequality Across Generations
    Gallipoli, G., Low, H., & Mitra, A.
    Forthcoming, Journal of Labor Economics, 2025
    We document how inequality in consumption and income has evolved across generations born at different points in time. We show that generational differences in income risk, family transfers, and access to credit markets all contribute to the observed cross-cohort patterns.
  • Eliciting the Marginal Propensity to Consume in Surveys
    Crossley, T. F., Fisher, P., Levell, P., & Low, H.
    2026  ·  Chicago Fed WP 2026-04
    Chicago Fed Chicago Fed PDF PDF
    We develop and test survey-based methods to measure the marginal propensity to consume (MPC). We compare hypothetical survey responses to actual spending behavior and evaluate the reliability of different elicitation approaches for capturing heterogeneity in MPCs across households.
  • Saving on a Rainy Day, Borrowing for a Rainy Day
    Alan, S., Crossley, T., & Low, H.
    IFS and Cambridge Working Papers, 2015
    We study the precautionary motive for saving and borrowing. We show that households save in anticipation of future income uncertainty but also borrow when facing current income shortfalls, and that these two behaviors are consistent with a single model of consumption smoothing under uncertainty.
  • Using a Temporary Indirect Tax Cut as a Fiscal Stimulus: Evidence from the UK
    Crossley, T. F., Low, H., O'Dea, C., & Sleeman, C.
    IFS Working Paper, 2016
    We evaluate the UK's 2008–2009 temporary VAT reduction as a fiscal stimulus. Using consumer expenditure data, we estimate the extent to which the tax cut was passed through to prices and assess the resulting impact on household consumption and saving.

PhD Research Topics

Life-Cycle Models with Uncertainty

Structural estimation of savings, labor supply, and investment decisions over the life cycle under uninsurable income, health, and family risks.

Social Insurance & Disability

Design and targeting of disability insurance programs, including screening errors and gender differences in access.

Consumption, Saving & Borrowing

Precautionary saving motives, the marginal propensity to consume, and household responses to income shocks.

Aggregate Implications of Household Behavior

How household heterogeneity in income risk and balance sheets shapes macroeconomic outcomes and policy transmission.

Economic Consequences of Divorce

Effects of marital dissolution on labor supply, consumption, migration, and child outcomes.

Labour Supply over the Life Cycle

Interactions between housing tenure, family formation, and labor supply across house price cycles, tax policy, and social insurance.
